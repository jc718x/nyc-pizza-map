The Definitive List

NYC Pizza Worth Traveling For

Some pizza is worth ordering because it's on the corner. This pizza is worth getting on a train for.

Most of the pizza on this map earns its place because it's genuinely good and it's near you. This list is different — these are the spots worth crossing the city for, even if it means a 45-minute subway ride and a wait once you get there. Coal ovens that have been running for decades, a Sicilian square that's been the same recipe since the 1930s, a converted candy store with a line down the block by 2pm. These are the ones that made me who I am when it comes to pizza.

Fifteen spots, all five boroughs, ranked by nothing but how much I'd tell a friend to go out of their way for it.

01
Lucali
BrooklynNeapolitan

A converted Carroll Gardens candy store turned into one of the most sought-after coal-oven pies in the city.

 02
Di Fara Pizza
BrooklynClassic Slice

Midwood institution run by the same family for decades — the standard every other classic slice gets measured against.

 03
Grimaldi's
BrooklynNeapolitan

Coal-oven pies in the shadow of the Brooklyn Bridge, running since 1990 in DUMBO.

 04
Juliana's
BrooklynNeapolitan

Patsy Grimaldi's return to the exact space he first started in — regulars still debate which DUMBO coal-oven pie wins.

 05
Totonno's
BrooklynNeapolitan

Coney Island coal-oven pizza dating back to 1924, still run by the founding family.

 06
L&B Spumoni Gardens
BrooklynSicilian

Gravesend's Sicilian square with sauce on top of the cheese — a genuinely different, unforgettable format.

 07
Krispy Pizza
BrooklynClassic Slice

A Dyker Heights slice counter that earns its name with a properly crisped crust, deep in one of Brooklyn's most Italian-American neighborhoods.

 08
Joe's Pizza
ManhattanClassic Slice

The Carmine Street original — the classic thin NY slice, done about as well as it gets.

 09
Lombardi's
ManhattanNeapolitan

America's first licensed pizzeria, opened in 1905 — still slinging coal-fired pies in Little Italy.

 10
Una Pizza Napoletana
ManhattanNeapolitan

A short, obsessive menu from a pizzaiolo who does exactly one thing and does it better than almost anyone.

 11
John's of Bleecker Street
ManhattanNeapolitan

Coal-oven West Village institution since 1929 — no slices, booth-carved initials, old New York in every sense.

 12
New Park Pizza
QueensClassic Slice

Howard Beach's answer to the classic slice — locals will tell you it's worth the trip to the edge of Queens.

 13
Joe & Pat's
Staten IslandClassic Slice

Staten Island's most-cited answer to "why would you go to Staten Island for pizza."

 14
Lee's Tavern
Staten IslandBar pie

An old-school Staten Island bar pie spot that's been a locals' secret for generations.

 15
Zero Otto Nove
BronxNeapolitan

Arthur Avenue's serious Neapolitan pie, in the heart of the Bronx's Little Italy.
