Best Pizza in Manhattan

Manhattan is where American pizza began, and it still carries the weight of that. Coal ovens downtown, counter slices sold to people who never sit down, and a dense run of wood-fired rooms that opened in the last twenty years — all packed into an island you can cross on foot.

Worth the Trip in Manhattan (4 spots)

Joe's Pizza $ Classic Slice ⭐ Worth a special trip

The Greenwich Village original, open since 1975. No frills, fold-and-go, and about as close to a citywide consensus pick for classic NY slice as exists.
John's of Bleecker Street $$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

A West Village institution since 1929, with a coal oven that turns out thin, blistered whole pies only — no slices, no delivery. The original booths and brick walls have barely changed.
Lombardi's $$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

Widely recognized as America's first pizzeria, open since 1905 in Little Italy. Coal-oven pies served whole (no slices), with fresh mozzarella and San Marzano tomato simplicity that makes the history feel earned.
Una Pizza Napoletana $$$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

Anthony Mangieri's obsessively focused Lower East Side operation: a short fixed menu of wood-fired Neapolitan pies, open only a few days a week, with a national reputation built over decades.

About Pizza in Manhattan

Lombardi's opened on Spring Street in 1905, the first pizzeria in the country, and the trade spread outward from those few blocks. What Manhattan does better than anywhere is the slice built for someone in a hurry: thin, foldable, eaten standing up. The West Village and the East Village hold the highest concentration of places worth crossing town for.
