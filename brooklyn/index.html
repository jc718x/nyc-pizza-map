Worth the Trip (7 Brooklyn spots)

The ones that justify a subway ride from anywhere in the city.

Di Fara Pizza $ Classic Slice ⭐ Worth a special trip

The benchmark by which a lot of NYC pizza gets judged. Founder Dom DeMarco built the reputation over decades; the family runs it today with the same hand-snipped basil finish.
Grimaldi's $$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

A DUMBO fixture since 1990, cooking coal-oven pies with a thin, blistered crust in the shadow of the Brooklyn Bridge. One of the pizzerias most credited with starting the modern NYC pizza pilgrimage.
Juliana's $$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

Opened in 2012 by Patsy Grimaldi in the very space where he first started. Same coal oven, same thin char-edged crust; regulars debate endlessly which DUMBO neighbor is better.
Krispy Pizza $ Classic Slice ⭐ Worth a special trip

A Dyker Heights neighborhood pizzeria on 13th Avenue, in the heart of one of Brooklyn's most Italian-American communities. The name says it all — known for a well-crisped crust and reliable classic NY-style slices.
L&B Spumoni Gardens – Gravesend $ Sicilian ⭐ Worth a special trip

A Gravesend institution since the late 1930s, known for its upside-down square slices — sauce on top, cheese on the bottom — and the spumoni ice cream that gives the place its name.
Lucali $$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

A converted candy store turned into one of the city's most sought-after coal-oven pies. No slices, cash only, and a line that starts before opening — reserve ahead or plan to wait.
Totonno's $$ Neapolitan ⭐ Worth a special trip

Open since 1924 in Coney Island, one of the oldest coal-oven pizzerias in the country. Cash only, whole pies only, still run by the founder's descendants.
